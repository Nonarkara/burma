## How to use this knowledge

Separate four things in every decision:

1. **Source fact:** attributed to an institution, with a date and population scope.
2. **Product inference:** our proposed response to that evidence.
3. **Research hypothesis:** something to test with community members.
4. **Operational verification:** a service or opportunity checked for its current requirements, availability, and contact details.

Reading an organisation's website is not operational verification. It does not establish a referral partnership, confirm that a phone is answered, or prove that a shelter has space. No precise service pin should be published from an inferred coordinate.

Keep public service information separate from personal records. Never store immigration documents, employer allegations, shelter residents, chat identities, or individual social posts in this knowledge base. Do not collect ethnicity or immigration status to make the public directory usable.

## Maintenance contract

For each public card retain: source URL, publisher, source publication date if known, date checked, service population, location precision, languages actually confirmed, eligibility, cost status, deadline with timezone, and a short uncertainty statement. Unknown fields stay unknown.

Proposed review intervals are editorial policy, not evidence of freshness: emergency contacts monthly; service-directory entries quarterly; policy and deadlines before publication and whenever an official update arrives. Border operating status requires timestamped operational evidence with an explicit expiry; without it show “current status not confirmed.”

Expired calls stay in an archive, excluded from “open.” Announcements are not application calls. A primary source proves what that source states; it does not justify an unrestricted “Verified” badge on every related claim.

Corrections should append a dated change note and the superseding source. Preserve past rules as historical material rather than silently replacing their dates. Before release, a Burmese-native reviewer must assess wording, and a relevant service partner must confirm any referral flow represented as a partnership.
